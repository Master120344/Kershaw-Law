These answers provide general information, not legal advice. Requirements can change, and the rules that apply depend on the facts of your matter.

What is the difference between H-2A and H-2B?

H-2A covers agricultural labor or services of a temporary or seasonal nature. H-2B covers temporary nonagricultural employment. Both require the employer to satisfy labor certification requirements, including U.S. worker recruitment and protections for wages and working conditions.

The job duties and the employer's temporary need determine which program, if either, may be appropriate.

Does a labor shortage make an employer eligible?

No. An employer must also meet the program's requirements for the work and its temporary need. For H-2B, that need must fit a one-time occurrence, seasonal, peakload, or intermittent category. H-2A work must be agricultural and temporary or seasonal.

The employer must document the relevant facts and comply with recruitment and employment requirements.

What are the main stages of the process?

Generally, the employer completes the Department of Labor's temporary labor certification process, including required recruitment, and then files a petition with USCIS. For workers outside the United States, visa processing where required and admission are additional steps.

A labor certification is not a visa, and approval at one stage does not guarantee approval at the next.

How far in advance should we begin?

Begin planning well before the proposed start date. The programs have prescribed filing windows, recruitment requirements, and separate agency processes. The time needed to collect records, prepare filings, and complete later stages varies.

Consult the current H-2A filing guidance or H-2B filing guidance and discuss your proposed dates before committing to a hiring timetable.

What should we prepare for an initial discussion?

Have your proposed job duties, worksite locations, employment dates, number of workers, and wage information available. Records explaining the seasonal or temporary need, past staffing patterns, and any prior program filings are also useful.

The documents needed for a filing depend on the program and the employer's circumstances.

What responsibilities continue after approval?

Employers must comply with applicable wage and employment terms, keep required records, and meet the program's worker protection requirements. H-2A includes housing and transportation obligations for covered workers. H-2B has its own recruitment, wage, transportation, and other requirements.

Some protections also apply to U.S. workers in corresponding employment. Approval does not remove these obligations.

Can recruitment or legal costs be charged to workers?

Employers must not pass prohibited recruitment, labor certification, or related attorney and agent costs to workers. The precise cost and reimbursement rules depend on the program and the expense involved.

Review recruiter arrangements and deductions before workers incur costs. The Department of Labor's recruitment guidance explains important worker protections.

Can approval or a worker arrival date be guaranteed?

No. Government agencies make approval decisions. Processing times, visa availability, additional evidence requests, and admission procedures can affect the outcome and timing.

A case assessment can identify relevant requirements and risks; it cannot promise an approval or a specific arrival date.

Does sending an inquiry make us a client?

No. Sending a form, email, or other inquiry does not by itself establish an attorney-client relationship. Representation begins only after the firm agrees to accept a matter.

Keep an initial inquiry to general business and contact information. Do not send sensitive or confidential material before the firm confirms how it should be provided.
